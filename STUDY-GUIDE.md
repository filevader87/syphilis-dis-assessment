## Key Concepts Summary

### Staging Hierarchy (Acquired Syphilis)
1. **Primary** — chancre present, regional lymphadenopathy
2. **Secondary** — rash (palmar/plantar), condylomata lata, mucous patches; RPR typically ≥1:32
3. **Early Non-Primary Non-Secondary (NPNS)** — reactive serology + acquisition evidence within 12 months, no current primary/secondary signs
4. **Unknown Duration** — reactive serology, no qualifying acquisition evidence

### Critical Distinctions

**Secondary Path B vs Path A:**
- **Path B:** Clinical criteria + reactive nontreponemal ≥1:32 AND treponemal is absent/pending (not nonreactive)
- **Path A:** Clinical criteria + both reactive treponemal AND reactive nontreponemal
- A **nonreactive** treponemal result **disqualifies Path B** — the test was done and it's negative, unlike a pending/absent result

**Acquisition Evidence (what qualifies):**
- ✓ Documented negative serology within 12 months → seroconversion
- ✓ Patient-reported primary symptoms (chancre) within 12 months
- ✓ Epidemiologic linkage to a partner **independently documented** as a syphilis case
- ✗ Mere sexual contact within 12 months (partner must be documented)
- ✗ Provider treatment regimen (doesn't determine surveillance staging)
- ✗ RPR change outside 12 months (outside the acquisition window)

**Fourfold Titer Changes:**
- A **fourfold decrease** (1:16 → 1:2) = treatment response or natural decline, NOT new infection
- A **fourfold increase** (1:2 → 1:8) = supportive evidence of new/recent infection
- Direction matters — decrease ≠ increase

**Confirmatory Lab Tests (direct detection):**
- Darkfield microscopy
- Immunohistochemistry (IHC)
- NAAT (nucleic acid amplification test)
- NOT: EIA, TP-PA (these are screening/treponemal, not direct detection)

### Congenital Syphilis
- Reported by **infant's place of birth** (2026 change)
- **Probable Congenital Syphilis:** untreated or inadequately treated maternal syphilis + infant serologic evidence
- NOT probable if: adequate maternal treatment + appropriate titer decline + infant RPR not fourfold higher

### Sub-classification (Ocular/Neurologic)
- **Verified:** Requires direct detection from affected site (aqueous/vitreous fluid for ocular, CSF for neurologic with specific criteria)
- **Likely:** Clinical criteria met but no specimen for direct detection
- CSF RPR can support **Verified** neurologic syphilis (new in 2026 framework) when specimen is not blood-contaminated

### Re-staging
- Staging can be **updated** when new information becomes available
- Patient-reported primary symptoms within 12 months = valid acquisition evidence for re-staging to Early NPNS

### Laboratory Evidence Tiers
- **Confirmatory:** Direct detection methods (darkfield, IHC, NAAT)
- **Presumptive:** Reactive treponemal OR reactive nontreponemal (single type)
- **Supportive:** Fourfold titer change indicating recent acquisition
